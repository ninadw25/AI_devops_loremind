## 3. Stale Deleted Channel Discovery

**Description**

Deleted or no-longer-visible Slack channels may continue to appear in discovery and ingestion outputs.

**Current Behavior**

* Stale channels can appear in ACL artifacts (`scope_principals.json`).
* Stale channels may appear during:

```bash
python -m infra.slack.run --org ezobooks
```

* No new knowledge is typically generated for such channels.
* Graph queries return no active KnowledgeNodes for those channels.

**Observed Example**

```text
#work-to-do
```

appears in discovery/ingestion output even though the channel is no longer present in the active Slack workspace.

**Impact**

ACL propagation for active channels continues to work correctly. This issue only affects stale channel metadata.



## 4. Known Limitation — Unassigned Task Validation

Tasks extracted from Slack messages may be rejected during validation if they do not contain an `ASSIGNED_TO` relationship.

For example, a message such as:

> "Complete Slack ACL verification by Friday."

is a valid task but does not explicitly assign ownership to any individual. The current validation logic requires every Task to have at least one `ASSIGNED_TO` link and rejects tasks that do not satisfy this condition with:

```text
orphan_task_no_ASSIGNED_TO_link
```

As a result, unassigned or team-level tasks may be removed during validation and will not be materialized as KnowledgeNodes, even though the task is successfully extracted.

This limitation affects task extraction quality only and does not impact Slack ACL discovery, scope propagation, citation generation, or ACL enforcement.

## 5. Known Limitation — Inconsistent Fallback Organization Handling

During decision extraction, the knowledge extraction layer may generate different fallback organization representations when the actor of a decision cannot be confidently identified from the source text.

For example, semantically similar statements may produce:

```text
Unknown Organization --DECIDED--> <Decision>
```

or

```text
UNKNOWN --DECIDED--> <Decision>
```

The validation layer currently treats placeholder values such as `UNKNOWN` as invalid entities and rejects the associated triple, while `Unknown Organization` may be accepted. As a result, some valid decision statements can be rejected depending on which fallback representation is generated by the extraction layer.

Example:

Accepted:

> "We are standardizing extraction on Gemini 2.5 Flash."

Rejected:

> "We decided to migrate Slack ACL validation to the unified builder."

In the rejected case, removal of the `DECIDED` relationship causes the corresponding Decision node to become orphaned and subsequently fail structural validation.

This limitation originates in the knowledge extraction and validation pipeline and is not related to Slack ingestion, ACL discovery, scope propagation, citation generation, or ACL enforcement.
